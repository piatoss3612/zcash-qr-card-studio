Use case: a character-design option sheet for a new embed Vizorcat, the Patchwork Jester — a cheeky, lovable clown whose whole outfit is sewn from big bold patches. Draw exactly three full-body variations of the SAME character in the SAME pose side by side on one landscape canvas (1536 × 1024), left to right 1–3, with only small natural differences between them, clearly separated with wide empty space between them, each turned three-quarters toward screen-left with its head and gaze toward screen-left.

Proportions come FIRST. The jester must be built on the same chubby chibi body as the print Vizorcats in references 2–4 (Classic Guardian, Samurai, Grove Ranger, Wandering Swordsman):
- The head (chin to the top of the ears, without the hood points) is as tall as the entire body below the chin — a BIG head. Head and body are each about half of the figure's height; the body is not taller than the head.
- The head is about 1.2 times as wide as the shoulders; big round eyes.
- A short, round, stocky body; very short stubby legs: the puffy patchwork shorts reach almost to the small round feet, so only a sliver of leg shows. No stockings.

The patchwork look (critical — match reference 1 exactly in style): every garment is made of BIG, bold, irregular squares and rectangles of cloth — each patch about a quarter of the hood's width — in warm orange, charcoal black, cream and faded olive green, joined by thick, clearly visible cream cross-stitches along every seam. Edges end in ragged, fringed strips that hang loose.

The character: a black-and-white "patched" bicolor cat — only black and white fur, irregular black patches on white, no orange or ginger fur anywhere — with a round black patch of fur around its left eye like a natural eye-patch marking, and bright green eyes. Both ears are normal, smooth and unnotched. Outfit: a single floppy pointed patchwork hood exactly like the one in reference 1 — a cone-shaped hood whose one long point droops to the side and ends in a cream tassel — with both cat ears coming out through it (no two-pointed jester cap); a short patchwork cape whose hem hangs in ragged strips; a patchwork tunic with a small ruffled cream collar and extra-long floppy patchwork sleeves that hang past the paws and completely cover both paws (like a Pierrot's sleeves); patched puffy shorts; small round patchwork booties. A cheerful, cheeky clown — friendly, never scary; no face paint, no red nose.

The pose (all three): the balancing act — standing with both feet on top of a small patchwork ball, knees slightly bent, both arms out to the sides at shoulder height with the long floppy sleeves drooping and covering both paws, tail up for balance, tongue poking out in happy concentration, head and gaze toward screen-left.

References (attached in this order):
1. Crops of an earlier patchwork concept (the face and instrument are erased) — the patch style to match: big patches, thick cross-stitches, ragged strip hem. Do not copy its coat colours.
2. The Vizorcat proportion reference — the body proportions to match exactly.
3–4. The print Samurai and Classic Guardian — the finished drawing style (outline weight, stepped pixel-art-adjacent edges, cel shading, rich detail) and body proportions.

Exactly two cat ears, each drawn once at normal length; the hood's point is clearly cloth with a tassel, not an ear. Non-sexual chibi animal mascot.
