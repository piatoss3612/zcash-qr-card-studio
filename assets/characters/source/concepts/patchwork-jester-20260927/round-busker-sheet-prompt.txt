Use case: a character-design option sheet for a new embed Vizorcat, the Patchwork Busker — a cheerful street performer whose whole outfit is patched together from mismatched cloth squares with visible stitches. Draw exactly three full-body characters side by side on one landscape canvas (1536 × 1024), left to right 1–3, clearly separated with empty space between them, each turned three-quarters toward screen-left.

Each option is a DIFFERENT cat with its own memorable identity (a natural cat coat plus one permanent feature), all wearing the same kind of patchwork street-performer outfit: a floppy patchwork cap or a patchwork hood with both ears out, a scarf, a short patchwork cape or vest with a ragged hem, patched trousers and worn little boots, in faded green, rust, mustard, cream and denim-blue patches with stitched seams (reference 1 shows the patch style only). No calico or tortoiseshell coats. Beside each cat's feet on the ground sits its own empty upturned patched felt tip hat — empty, no coins, no sign, no text.

1 — Mime busker: a black-and-white "patched" bicolor cat with irregular black patches on white, a small notch in the tip of one ear (a street cat's mark) and green eyes. Both paws pressed flat against an invisible pane of glass in front of it, the pad sides facing the viewer, with a cheeky, wide-eyed "oh no, a wall!" face.
2 — Harmonica busker: a solid cream (buff) cat with a freckled pink nose and amber eyes. A small harmonica held at its mouth by a thin wire neck rack (no paw touches it), cheeks puffed as it plays, both paws raised beside the shoulders clapping the beat, pad sides facing the viewer.
3 — Whistling busker: a solid chocolate-brown cat with a small white star on its chest and green eyes. Both paws tucked into the front pockets of its patched trousers (both arms clearly visible, both paws inside the pockets), whistling a tune with puckered lips, leaning back in a jaunty stance.
